RVSF Machinery Checklist: Every Machine You Need to Set Up a Vehicle Scrapping Facility in India

Most people planning a Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility start with the licence. That is the wrong end to start from. The licence application asks you to describe your plant — the de-pollution bay, the storage areas, the processing equipment, the effluent handling. If you have not decided your RVSF machinery first, you are filling in that form with guesses, and guesses become the specification you are later inspected against.

This checklist works the other way round. It walks through the equipment an RVSF actually needs, machine by machine, in the order material moves through the yard — so you can size your plant before you commit to it on paper.

Why the machinery list matters more than it used to

India's vehicle scrappage framework has moved from policy to enforcement. Under the Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules, 2021, only a registered facility can issue a Certificate of Deposit — and the CoD is what unlocks the incentives that make scrapping worthwhile for the vehicle owner. Under MoRTH's notification, state governments have been advised to offer a waiver of up to 25% on motor vehicle tax for private vehicles purchased against a valid CoD, with a lower band for commercial vehicles.

Separate concessions on registration charges and GST have also been announced against a valid CoD, though the exact benefit varies by state and vehicle category — check your state transport department's current notification rather than relying on a national figure.

That structure funnels end-of-life vehicles toward registered facilities specifically. Meanwhile the supply side is enormous and the processing capacity is not: industry estimates put India's end-of-life vehicle population in the tens of millions over the coming decade, against a registered facility base still numbering in the low hundreds.

The gap is the opportunity. But it is only an opportunity if your plant can actually process throughput, and that comes down to machine selection.

Stage 1 — Inbound handling and storage

Before anything is cut or crushed, vehicles have to be received, recorded and staged.

What you need:

  • Weighbridge — non-negotiable. Every CoD is tied to a recorded weight.
  • Covered impervious storage for vehicles awaiting de-pollution, with spill containment.
  • Material handler or forklift with a grab or fork attachment for moving hulks.

This stage is civil work and handling equipment rather than process machinery, but under-sizing it is the most common early mistake. A yard that can process 20 vehicles a day but can only stage 12 spends the difference in idle time.

Stage 2 — De-pollution

This is the stage the regulator cares about most, and the one that most distinguishes a registered facility from an informal yard.

What you need:

  • De-pollution bay with an impervious floor, bunding and oil-water separator.
  • Fluid extraction system for engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, transmission fluid, fuel and AC refrigerant — each into a separately labelled, sealed container.
  • Airbag deployment station and pyrotechnic handling protocol.
  • Battery and tyre removal area with segregated storage.
  • Effluent treatment appropriate to your state pollution control board's consent conditions.

De-pollution equipment is comparatively low-capital, but it is the hard gate in your licence inspection. Budget for it properly.

Stage 3 — Dismantling and reusable parts recovery

Once de-polluted, a vehicle is worth more taken apart than crushed whole — at least the parts of it that are.

What you need:

  • Vehicle lifts or tilting rigs so operators can work underneath safely.
  • Hand tools, impact wrenches, cutting torches.
  • Parts cleaning, testing and racking if you intend to sell reusable components.
  • Nibblers for cutting sheet panels with minimal deformation, where you want to recover clean sheet rather than deform it.

This is where margin sits for many operators. Catalytic converters, alloy wheels, gearboxes, ECUs and body panels frequently return more than their scrap weight.

Stage 4 — Size reduction and cutting

Now the hulk has to become handleable material.

What you need:

  • Alligator shear — the workhorse for cutting axles, propeller shafts, chassis rails, suspension arms, cable and tube. Look at three numbers: shear force, jaw opening and strokes per minute. A machine in the 85–200 ton band with a 12–16 inch jaw opening will cut 85–120 mm round stock at 7–12 strokes a minute, which is the range most ELV work sits in.
  • Inclined shear or shear-baler if you want cutting and baling in one pass.
  • Shredder — a single-shaft or twin-shaft shredder for bulky, mixed or low-density material, where you need consistent output size for downstream separation.

The shear-versus-shredder decision usually comes down to what you sell. If your buyer wants sized, sorted, furnace-ready material, a shredder plus separation earns the premium. If your buyer takes dense bundles, a shear and baler combination gets you there with far less capital and power draw.

Stage 5 — Compaction and baling

This is the machine most people picture when they think of a scrapping facility, and it is the one that decides your freight economics.

What you need:

  • Car baler (ELV compactor) — purpose-built to compact whole car bodies, doors, and bus or truck panels into solid, high-density bundles. This is the defining machine of an ELV line.
  • High-density baler for mixed ferrous and non-ferrous scrap generated across the yard. Cylinder forces up to 650 tons handle everything from light aluminium to heavy TMT offcuts, with bale sizes from 18×18" to 40×40".
  • Super jumbo baler if you are running high volumes of heavy or oversized scrap — 300-ton cylinder force, 2–10 tons per hour, bale cycles under 75 seconds.

Bale density is not a vanity metric. Freight is charged by volume as often as by weight, and a loose load costs you the same truck as a dense one while carrying materially less material. Compaction is the single largest lever on your cost per tonne shipped.

Stage 6 — Separation, conveying and non-ferrous recovery

What you need:

  • Belt or chain conveyors to move material between stations without manual handling.
  • Magnetic separation for ferrous recovery post-shredding.
  • Eddy current separator for non-ferrous — this is where aluminium, copper and zinc recovery pays.
  • Sorting line or picking station for residual mixed fractions.

Conveying is routinely under-budgeted. A yard that moves material with a loader between every station burns diesel, operator hours and floor space that a fixed conveyor would have saved in the first year.

Stage 7 — Non-metallic residue

Automotive shredder residue, glass, plastics and upholstery all have to go somewhere documented.

What you need:

  • Vertical baler for light dry waste — plastics, upholstery fabric, foam and cardboard — turning loose volume into stackable, transportable blocks.
  • Segregated storage with disposal or downstream-recycler agreements in place.

Two points worth stressing. First, this is a starting configuration, not a template — scrap mix, buyer requirements and site footprint all move it. Second, no serious plant runs on machine selection alone. Layout, power availability, foundation requirements and operator training determine whether the machines you bought actually deliver their rated throughput. Several of the machines above need no civil foundation, which materially shortens commissioning time and keeps relocation possible.

Before you file the licence application

Confirm the following are settled:

  1. Target vehicles per day and per year, with a realistic ramp.
  2. Output form your buyers actually want — bundles, sized shred, or sorted fractions.
  3. Site layout with material flow drawn end to end.
  4. Connected load and power availability against total motor HP.
  5. Consent-to-establish conditions from your state pollution control board.
  6. After-sales cover — spares availability and AMC terms — for every process machine.

That last one deserves more weight than it usually gets. Downtime on a single baler in a single-line yard stops the whole facility. Confirm spares inventory and response times before you sign, not after.

Frequently asked questions

What machinery is mandatory for an RVSF licence in India? The Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules, 2021 require de-pollution infrastructure, impervious flooring with spill containment, segregated storage and a weighbridge as core conditions. Processing machinery such as shears, balers and shredders is sized to your intended throughput rather than mandated at a fixed specification. Confirm current requirements with your state transport authority and pollution control board before applying.

Do I need both a shear and a shredder? Not necessarily. A shear plus a baler is the lower-capital route and suits operators selling dense bundles. A shredder earns its cost where buyers pay a premium for sized, separated, furnace-ready material and where volumes justify the power draw.

Does a car baler need a civil foundation? It depends on the machine. Several baler configurations — including double-action and vertical presses — require no civil foundation, which shortens commissioning and allows later relocation. Confirm foundation requirements per model at the quotation stage.

How long does it take to commission an ELV plant? Timelines vary with site readiness, power connection and regulatory approvals rather than machine delivery. Sequencing the licence application alongside machinery procurement, instead of one after the other, is what compresses the schedule.
