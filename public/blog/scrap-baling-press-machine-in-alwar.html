Scrap Baling Press Machine in Alwar: Sizing One for the Alwar–Bhiwadi Scrap Mix

If you are looking for a scrap baling press machine in Alwar, the question that decides whether the purchase works is not who to buy from. It is what you are going to feed the machine, and who is going to buy the bale at the other end. Get those two answers written down and the specification almost fills itself in. Get them wrong and you end up with a press that runs beautifully on somebody else's scrap.

We wrote separately about how to evaluate a baler machine manufacturer in Alwar — the factory-visit and supplier-vetting side. This article is the other half: specifying and siting the press itself for the material and the buyers that actually exist in this belt.

The scrap that moves through this corridor

Alwar sits between the Delhi-NCR industrial belt and Jaipur, adjacent to the Bhiwadi–Neemrana automotive cluster. That geography shapes the scrap stream in three ways worth planning around.

Automotive and sheet-metal offcuts. Component manufacturing produces press-shop offcuts, skeletons and punchings — light-gauge, high-volume, awkward to transport loose. This is the classic baling application and the reason most yards here buy a press at all.

Turnings, swarf and machining waste. Machine shops produce it continuously. It is abrasive, it holds coolant, and it is the worst material there is for a chamber liner. If it is clean single-alloy material going back to a furnace, it is often better briquetted than baled — a vertical briquetting press compresses chips, filings, shavings and swarf into uniform briquettes with volume reduction up to 90%, a different economic proposition to a bale.

Rolling-mill and furnace feed. Rolling mills and induction furnaces in this region and the wider Rajasthan–Punjab corridor buy compacted scrap by weight and by how it charges. This is the demand side, and the constraint most first-time buyers ignore.

The policy backdrop points the same way. India is in the final stage of formulating a national steel scrap recycling policy; the Ministry of Steel has indicated total scrap availability is expected to reach nearly 36 million tonnes, with an ambition to lift scrap usage toward the global average of around 31% (Yieh Corp Steel News, January 2026). Those are intentions and projections rather than settled fact — read the final text when it is published. But the direction, toward more formal processing and more demand for consistent furnace-grade feed, is reasonably clear.

Ask your buyer for the bale size before you ask for a quotation

This is the single most useful thing in this article, and almost nobody does it.

A bale is not a generic product. A mill or furnace has a charging window — the physical opening, the crane or magnet capacity, the charge weight it wants per drop. A bale that is 50 mm too wide is worthless to that buyer no matter how dense it is. One that is right in every dimension but weighs 900 kg when they want 300 kg forces them to break it.

So before you specify, call two or three buyers you expect to sell to and ask what bale dimensions and weight they accept, and what they pay differently for. Then match against the range. The JHP catalogue specifies, for mild steel:

Two things to read off that table. First, bale weight for the same nominal bale size varies enormously with material — the Triple Action Baler at 20×20 inches is specified at 200–350 kg in mild steel but 65–100 kg in aluminium. Density is a property of what you bale, not of the press. Second, cycle time and bale weight together give you throughput, and throughput is what you should be buying, not press force.

Matching the press to the material — honestly

Four cases where the obvious answer is the wrong one.

Light aluminium sections and UBCs at modest volume. You do not need a 650-ton press. The Mini Triple Action Baler produces 6×6 to 10×10 inch bales at up to 10 tons per hour on a 20–25 HP motor with a compact footprint. Buying larger means paying for cylinder force you will never use and running a bigger motor to do it.

Heavy TMT bar and oversized structural scrap. Here the tonnage genuinely matters. The Super Jumbo Baler is specified to process heavy TMT bars of 5–10 mm thickness with cylinder force up to 300 tons, production of 2–10 tons per hour and a bale in under 75 seconds. The High Density Baler goes to cylinder force up to 650 tons with bales from 18×18 to 40×40 inches. Below those, heavy bar will simply not compact.

Mixed light scrap with occasional oversized pieces. The honest answer is often two machines, not one bigger one. An alligator shear ahead of the baler — the range covers 35–200 tons of shear force, jaw openings of 9–16 inches and cutting capacity from 40 mm up to 120 mm round — sizes the awkward pieces so a smaller press handles everything. Two right-sized machines usually beat one oversized press on capital and running cost both.

Turnings and swarf as the main feed. Consider whether you want bales at all. Clean single-alloy material briquettes into a denser, more consistent product with lower melting losses. Mixed or contaminated material should be baled — but budget for liner wear from day one and specify the harder plate grade. The High Density Baler and Super Jumbo Baler are specified with HARDOX 500-BHN wear plates and compression chamber; SSAB specifies Hardox 500 at a nominal 500 HBW, through-hardened, with minimum core hardness at 90% of the guaranteed minimum (SSAB).

Site, power and foundation — sort this out before delivery

Three practical items that delay commissioning far more often than the machine does.

Foundation. Not every press needs one, and it matters for both cost and flexibility. In the JHP catalogue the three Double Action variants — auto door, manual door and top ejection — and the Vertical Baler are all specified as requiring no civil foundation, which shortens installation and lets you relocate the press later. The alligator shear and hydraulic nibbler are likewise specified as needing no civil foundation. Larger presses differ; confirm the requirement for the exact model quoted, in writing, before you pour anything.

Sanctioned load. Motor ratings in the range run from 10 HP on a small vertical baler to 80/100/120 HP on the largest High Density and Super Jumbo models, with the twin shaft shredder at 40×2 to 75×2 HP. Check your sanctioned load against the machine's full-load draw plus starting current, and check your industrial-area supply position early — a load enhancement application is not a same-week process.

Material flow and clearance. Decide how scrap reaches the chamber and how the bale leaves before the machine arrives. The High Density Baler offers top, turn-out and side ejection options, and the Double Action Baler comes in a top-ejection configuration specifically for sites where side or front clearance is limited. Conveyors are supplied at 900 mm belt width in inclined magnetic, flat-bed sorting and transfer types at 3,700–4,000 mm lengths, with custom lengths and inclinations available. Getting ejection direction wrong is expensive to correct once the foundation is set.

If you are setting up an RVSF in Rajasthan

Vehicle scrapping is a distinct regulatory track, not just a bigger baler. Rajasthan's Transport and Road Safety Department publishes a compiled reference bringing together the MoRTH Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules notifications — GSR 653(E) of 23 September 2021, GSR 695(E) of 13 September 2022 and GSR 212(E) of 15 March 2024 — alongside its own departmental office orders 13/2022, 4/2023 and 17/2023 (Transport Department, Rajasthan).

Several requirements in that framework affect your site and equipment decisions directly. The applicant must demonstrate availability of sufficient usable land in an orange-category industrial area, through ownership, a sale agreement, or a lease of at least three years. There is a commitment to obtain consent to operate from the State Pollution Control Board within six months of commencing operations, and to obtain ISO 9001, ISO 14001 or ISO 45001 certification within twelve months. The state is expected to process a proposal within 60 days, including internal clearances from labour, pollution and other agencies.

Rules and departmental orders change, and interpretation varies by RTO. Treat the above as a starting point and confirm the current position with the Rajasthan Transport Department and your regional RTO before committing to a site or an equipment order.

On machinery, the catalogue sets out the line typically deployed in ELV/RVSF plants: a car baler compacting entire vehicle bodies into high-density bundles, inclined or box shear for car bodies and heavy structural vehicle scrap, alligator shear for round bar, channel, TMT and structural parts, high density and triple action balers for body panels, doors and mixed ELV scrap, a twin shaft shredder for pre-conditioning bulky components, and a casting cracker for engines and gearboxes at 3–5 tons per hour. Car baler specifications are quoted against the plant, not from a standard table.

What buying locally actually gets you

Proximity is worth something concrete and worth nothing vague.

It genuinely helps with: freight on a machine that may weigh several tonnes, a service engineer who can reach you in hours rather than days, spares dispatched the same day, and the ability to walk into the factory with a sack of your own scrap and watch it get baled before you commit. That last one is the real advantage.

It does not substitute for: in-house hydraulics, named wear-plate grades, a spares store you can see, and a trial on your material. A supplier twenty minutes away who buys in complete hydraulic assemblies is a worse bet than a good manufacturer four hundred kilometres away. Location is convenience, not credentials — which is what the manufacturer evaluation checklist is for.
