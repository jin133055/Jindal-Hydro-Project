GST on Scrap Metal in India: The Rate, Reverse Charge and the 2% TDS Rule

Ask three people in a scrap yard what the gst rate on scrap metal india applies is and you may get three answers. The confusion is understandable: the headline rate is only part of the picture, and since October 2024 two additional rules — reverse charge and a 2% TDS deduction — have changed how the tax actually moves through the trade.

This article sets out what those rules say, who they apply to, and what they mean in practice for a yard, a recycler or a plant buying scrap. It is written for operators, not accountants. Tax law is amended frequently, so treat this as orientation and confirm your own position with your chartered accountant or against the CBIC rate finder before acting on it.

The headline rate

Metal scrap generally attracts GST at 18%. The relevant goods sit in Chapters 72 to 81 of the First Schedule to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975 — iron and steel and articles thereof, copper, nickel, aluminium, lead, zinc, tin and other base metals.

That 18% is the starting point. Who pays it, and how, depends on the registration status of both parties to the transaction.

The HSN chapters that matter

Chapter Covers
72 Iron and steel
73 Articles of iron and steel
74 Copper and articles thereof
75 Nickel and articles thereof
76 Aluminium and articles thereof
78 Lead and articles thereof
79 Zinc and articles thereof
80, 81 Tin, and other base metals

Notification No. 06/2024-Central Tax (Rate) lists Chapters "72, 73, 74, 75, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80 or 81" for the reverse charge entry. Classify your material to the correct heading — the chapter determines whether these rules bite at all.

Reverse charge: buying scrap from an unregistered seller

This is the change that caught the trade off guard. Under Notification No. 06/2024-Central Tax (Rate) dated 8 October 2024, which amends the original reverse charge notification of June 2017, metal scrap under those chapters was added to the reverse charge list. It came into force on 10 October 2024.

The effect: if a registered buyer receives metal scrap from an unregistered supplier, the buyer pays the GST, not the seller. The recommendation behind it, from the 54th GST Council, is explicit that the recipient pays under reverse charge even where the supplier is below the registration threshold.

Two practical consequences follow, and the second is the one that hurts.

You must self-assess and pay. The liability is yours as buyer, and it does not depend on the seller issuing anything.

It is a working capital cost. Tax under reverse charge can only be paid through the electronic cash ledger — you cannot discharge it using existing input tax credit. Credit for what you pay is available afterwards, but the cash goes out first. For a yard buying continuously from small unregistered collectors, that timing gap is real money tied up.

The 2% TDS rule on registered-to-registered supplies

Separately, and on the same date, TDS under Section 51 of the CGST Act was extended to metal scrap. Notification No. 25/2024-Central Tax dated 9 October 2024 amended the 2018 TDS notification so that a registered person receiving metal scrap under Chapters 72 to 81 from another registered person must deduct TDS at 2%, effective 10 October 2024.

The mechanics:

  • Rate: 1% CGST plus 1% SGST intra-state, or 2% IGST inter-state.
  • Threshold: no deduction where the total value of supply under a contract does not exceed ₹2,50,000.
  • Valuation: excludes CGST, SGST, IGST and cess as shown on the invoice.
  • Registration: the deductor needs a separate registration as a deductor.
  • Payment: remitted within 10 days after the end of the month of deduction.
  • Return: filed in GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month, with interest at 1.5% per month on delay.
  • The deductee's side: credit lands in the supplier's electronic cash ledger once the return is filed.

Which rule applies to you

The two rules are mutually exclusive — they turn on the registration status of each party.

Supplier Recipient TDS applies? Reverse charge applies?
Registered Registered Yes (above ₹2.5 lakh contract value) No
Registered Unregistered No No
Unregistered Registered No Yes
Unregistered Unregistered No No

The clearest way to read this: buying from an unregistered seller means you pay the tax; buying from a registered seller means you withhold 2% and remit it. Neither applies if you are not registered yourself.

There is also a location carve-out on the TDS side — no deduction where the supplier's location and the place of supply are both in a different state from the recipient's state of registration. That one catches people out on inter-state deals; check it with your CA.

What GST 2.0 changed, and what it did not

The 56th GST Council meeting in September 2025 approved a substantial rationalisation of the slab structure, with the changes taking effect from 22 September 2025. The 12% and 28% slabs were removed, leaving a principal two-rate structure of 5% and 18%, with a 40% rate introduced for a narrow set of luxury and demerit goods.

For the scrap trade, the practical point is that the reform reorganised the slabs rather than rewriting the treatment of metal scrap specifically. The October 2024 reverse charge and TDS provisions are separate mechanisms and were not part of that rationalisation.

Because slab movements affect a very large number of headings, verify the rate for your exact HSN code against the CBIC rate finder for the date of supply. Do not rely on a rate quoted to you in a previous financial year — or on this article.

GST on the machinery — and the credit you can claim

If you are buying equipment rather than scrap, a different heading applies. Hydraulic presses classify under HSN heading 8462, with sub-heading 846291 covering hydraulic presses specifically, listed at 18%.

For a registered business, that 18% is not a sunk cost. Input tax credit on capital goods used in the course or furtherance of business is ordinarily available, subject to the usual conditions — a valid tax invoice, receipt of the goods, the supplier having paid and reported the tax, and no depreciation claimed on the tax component under the Income-tax Act. That last one is where people trip: you claim either the ITC or the depreciation on the GST portion, not both.

Build this into the capital budget rather than treating GST as an 18% surcharge on the machine, and ask your CA to confirm the position for your entity.

A practical compliance checklist for a scrap operation

  1. Map your supplier base by registration status. Collect and verify GSTINs — you cannot apply the right rule until you know which side of the table each supplier sits on.
  2. Register as a deductor if you buy metal scrap from registered suppliers above the threshold. This is a separate registration.
  3. Set the ₹2.5 lakh test at contract level, not invoice level. Read the wording with your CA.
  4. Forecast the reverse charge cash outflow. It is payable through the cash ledger — a funding requirement, not just a ledger entry.
  5. Diarise the dates. TDS remitted within 10 days of month end; GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month; 1.5% per month interest on delay.
  6. Classify material to the right HSN, and re-check rates each year — the chapter drives everything downstream, and slabs change.
  7. Keep e-way bill and weighbridge records aligned with your invoicing. Scrap has historically been an enforcement focus area, which is the stated reason both provisions exist.

None of this is exotic, but all of it is easier to set up once than to reconstruct under audit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the GST rate on scrap metal in India? Metal scrap under Chapters 72 to 81 generally attracts GST at 18%. Rates and classifications are amended from time to time, so confirm the rate applicable to your specific HSN code and date of supply on the CBIC rate finder or with your chartered accountant.

Who pays GST when I buy scrap from an unregistered seller? You do, as the registered recipient, under the reverse charge mechanism. This was introduced by Notification No. 06/2024-Central Tax (Rate) dated 8 October 2024 and took effect on 10 October 2024. The tax is payable through the electronic cash ledger, so plan for the cash outflow.

When does the 2% TDS on metal scrap apply? When a registered person buys metal scrap under Chapters 72 to 81 from another registered person, and the total value of supply under the contract exceeds ₹2,50,000. It is 1% CGST plus 1% SGST intra-state, or 2% IGST inter-state, and it is reported in GSTR-7.

Do reverse charge and TDS ever apply to the same transaction? No. Reverse charge applies where the supplier is unregistered and the recipient is registered. TDS applies where both parties are registered. They do not overlap.

Can I claim input tax credit on the GST I pay under reverse charge? Credit for tax paid under reverse charge is ordinarily available subject to the normal conditions, but the tax itself must be paid in cash first — you cannot set off existing credit against a reverse charge liability. That timing gap is a working capital cost worth forecasting.

What GST applies when I buy a baling press or shredder? Hydraulic presses fall under HSN heading 8462, with sub-heading 846291 covering hydraulic presses, listed at 18%. For a registered business, input tax credit on capital goods used in business is ordinarily available, provided you do not also claim depreciation on the tax component under the Income-tax Act.
