# Vehicle Scrapping Policy India 2026: What It Offers, and What It Means If You Are Building a Scrapping Facility

Most explainers on the **vehicle scrapping policy India 2026** are written for the person who owns an old car. This one is written for the person on the other side of the transaction — the operator deciding whether to put capital into a scrapping facility, and the fabricator or recycler wondering whether the policy is finally generating enough deal flow to justify the machinery.

The short answer: the incentive structure is real and reasonably well documented, the physical capacity to absorb the vehicles is not, and the gap between those two things is where the commercial case sits. Here is what the policy actually says, stripped of the promotional framing.

## What the policy actually is

There is no single document called the "vehicle scrappage policy." What exists is a bundle of rules, notifications and voluntary industry commitments that MoRTH now markets under the banner of the **Voluntary Vehicle Modernization Programme**.

Three pieces matter:

1. **The Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules, 2021**, which create the Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility (RVSF) as a licensed category and set the conditions it must meet.
2. **The Central Motor Vehicles (Twenty-fourth Amendment) Rules, 2021**, which created the motor vehicle tax concession against a scrapping certificate. Reporting at the time put these into force from 1 April 2022.
3. **Voluntary OEM discounts**, agreed between MoRTH and vehicle manufacturers, which sit on top of everything else.

The word "voluntary" is doing real work here. For private vehicles, nothing forces an owner to scrap. What the policy does is stack enough small financial advantages that scrapping becomes the rational choice for a vehicle that would otherwise fail a fitness test.

## The Certificate of Deposit is the whole mechanism

Every incentive in the system hangs off one document: the **Certificate of Deposit**, issued by a registered facility when it accepts a vehicle.

Without a CoD, an owner gets scrap value and nothing else. With one, they unlock the tax concession, the registration fee waiver and the manufacturer discount. And critically — only a *registered* facility can issue one.

That single fact is the entire economic argument for registration. An unregistered yard competes on scrap price alone. A registered facility competes on scrap price *plus* several thousand rupees of benefits it can hand the seller at no cost to itself. The CoD is also tradeable, which means it can be sold to someone other than the person who scrapped the vehicle.

## What the owner actually receives

This is where most coverage gets vague, so here are the documented figures.

**Motor vehicle tax concession.** MoRTH proposed up to 25% concession for non-transport (private) vehicles and up to 15% for transport vehicles against a scrapping certificate, with the concession valid for 15 years for non-transport and 8 years for transport vehicles. Implementation is by state, so the actual benefit in your state may differ — confirm the current notification with your state transport department rather than relying on the national headline figure.

**OEM discounts.** In August 2024, MoRTH announced discounts agreed with manufacturers against a Certificate of Deposit:

| Vehicle type | Discount offered |
|---|---|
| Commercial cargo, >3.5 t GVW, scrapped by owner in last 6 months | 3% of ex-showroom price |
| Commercial cargo, <3.5 t GVW, scrapped by owner in last 6 months | 1.5% of ex-showroom price |
| Commercial cargo, >3.5 t GVW, against a traded CoD | 2.75% of ex-showroom price |
| Commercial cargo, <3.5 t GVW, against a traded CoD | 1.25% of ex-showroom price |
| Passenger vehicle, scrapped by owner in last 6 months | 1.5% of ex-showroom price or ₹20,000, whichever is lower |

Mercedes-Benz India offered a flat ₹25,000 over and above existing discounts. The commercial vehicle commitment ran for two years and the passenger vehicle commitment for one, and individual manufacturers retained the liberty to apply their discount only to identified models. Check whether a given OEM offer is still live before quoting it to a customer.

**Registration fee waiver and scrap value.** A waiver of the fee for issuing a new certificate of registration applies against a CoD in many states, and the facility itself pays the owner scrap value for the vehicle.

Note what this adds up to for a mid-range car: a few tens of thousands of rupees against the price of a new vehicle. Meaningful, not transformative. Anyone building a business model on the assumption that owners will queue up because of the incentives alone is going to be disappointed. Volume comes from fitness-test failures and from commercial fleets, not from enthusiasm.

## The capacity gap is the actual opportunity

Here is the number that matters for an operator. As of MoRTH's August 2024 announcement, there were **60+ RVSFs across 17 states and union territories**, and 75+ Automated Testing Stations across 12. Industry trackers put the registered facility count somewhere in the 80–90 range by early 2026 — a secondary estimate, not an official figure, and worth verifying against the Vahan RVSF listing before you rely on it.

Set that against the volume of vehicles reaching end of life. Our own catalogue works from an estimate of roughly 28 million end-of-life vehicles in India by 2030. Even allowing for wide error bars on that projection, a facility base in the low hundreds cannot process it.

The distribution is as important as the count. Registered facilities cluster heavily around Delhi-NCR, the Mumbai region, Pune, Ahmedabad, Chennai and Bengaluru. Large parts of central, eastern and north-eastern India have thin coverage. Because hulks are expensive to transport — you are paying to move mostly air until the vehicle is compacted — catchment radius is a real constraint, and a facility in an underserved region faces less competition for inbound material than the headline national numbers suggest.

## What the policy quietly specifies about your plant

The rules do not hand you an equipment list. But the obligations they create push you toward a fairly specific configuration.

**You must record weight accurately**, because the CoD is tied to it. That means a calibrated weighbridge and disciplined record-keeping, not an estimate.

**You must de-pollute before dismantling**, which means an impervious bay, bunding, an oil-water separator and segregated, labelled fluid collection. This is the part of the inspection that facilities most often fail.

**You must move material out economically**, which is where compaction decides your margin. A loose hulk and a compacted bundle occupy very different volumes on the same truck, and freight is frequently the second-largest cost line after acquisition. A **car baler (ELV compactor)** is purpose-built for this — it compacts entire car bodies, doors and bus or truck panels into solid high-density bundles. Configuration and capacity are specified per project rather than sold off a fixed spec sheet.

Around it, a typical line uses an **alligator shear** for axles, propeller shafts and structural sections — machines in the 85–200 ton band with a 12–16 inch jaw opening cut 85–120 mm round stock at 7–12 strokes per minute — and a **high density baler** for the doors, panels and mixed yard scrap, with cylinder forces up to 650 tons and bale sizes from 18×18" to 40×40". Where engines and gearboxes need breaking down, a casting cracker does it; where bulky components need pre-conditioning, a twin shaft shredder handles it at low speed and high torque.

## Before you commit capital

Four things to settle, in this order:

1. **Confirm the current rules with your state transport authority and pollution control board.** Central rules set the frame; states set the tax concession, the consent conditions and much of the procedure. National summaries — including this one — go stale.
2. **Model your catchment, not the national ELV number.** Twenty-eight million vehicles nationally is irrelevant if your realistic radius yields eight vehicles a day.
3. **Decide your output form before your machine list.** Dense bundles, sized shred and sorted fractions each imply a different plant and a different buyer.
4. **Confirm spares and service cover.** In a single-line yard, one baler down stops the facility. Ask about parts inventory and response times before you sign.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is scrapping an old vehicle compulsory in India?**
For privately owned vehicles the programme is voluntary — MoRTH markets it as the Voluntary Vehicle Modernization Programme. What is not voluntary is fitness certification: a vehicle that fails its fitness test cannot legally be driven, which is what pushes most owners toward scrapping. Rules for government and commercial fleets differ and vary by state, so confirm your category with your state transport department.

**What is a Certificate of Deposit and who can issue it?**
It is the document a Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility issues when it accepts a vehicle for scrapping, and it is what unlocks the tax concession, registration fee waiver and OEM discount. Only a registered facility can issue one. It is also tradeable, so it can be transferred to a buyer other than the original owner.

**How much does an owner actually save by scrapping?**
It depends on the state, the vehicle category and the manufacturer. The documented components are scrap value from the facility, a motor vehicle tax concession of up to 25% for non-transport and up to 15% for transport vehicles, a registration fee waiver, and an OEM discount — for passenger vehicles, 1.5% of ex-showroom price or ₹20,000, whichever is lower. Verify each element against current state notifications and live OEM offers.

**Is there still room for new scrapping facilities, or is the market full?**
Registered capacity remains concentrated in a handful of metropolitan corridors, and large regions have very thin coverage. Whether that translates into a viable business depends entirely on your local catchment, buyer access and cost base — not on the national vehicle numbers. Model your own radius before assuming demand.

**Do I need a car baler, or will a general purpose baler do?**
If whole vehicle bodies are your main inbound material, a purpose-built car baler is the right machine — general purpose balers are sized for loose and cut scrap, not intact hulks. If you shear vehicles down first and bale the resulting sections, a high density baler may be sufficient. The decision follows your dismantling process, so settle that first.
