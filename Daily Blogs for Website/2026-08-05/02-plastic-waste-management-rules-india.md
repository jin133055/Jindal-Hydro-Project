# Plastic Waste Management Rules in India: What the 2026 Amendment Changes for Processors

Search **plastic waste management rules india** today and most of what you will find still describes the position before 31 March 2026. On that date the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change notified the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2026 through gazette notification G.S.R. 237(E), amending the principal 2016 rules and changing several things that matter directly to anyone who collects, bales, shreds or processes plastic waste.

This article covers what changed, who it now catches, and — honestly — which parts of it machinery can help with and which parts it cannot. Regulatory detail varies by state and is revised frequently, so treat this as an orientation and confirm your own position with your State Pollution Control Board or the CPCB before making decisions.

## What the amendment actually did

The 2026 Amendment Rules were notified under sections 3, 6 and 25 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, and came into force on publication in the Official Gazette. They followed a draft notification, G.S.R. 365(E), published on 3 June 2025 and a sixty-day consultation period.

Four changes stand out.

**Recycled content targets are now on a published escalator.** Producers, importers and brand owners must meet minimum recycled plastic content by packaging category:

| Category | 2025–26 | 2026–27 | 2027–28 | 2028–29 onwards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Category I — rigid packaging | 30% | 40% | 50% | 60% |
| Category II — flexible packaging | 10% | 10% | 20% | 20% |
| Category III — multi-layered | 5% | 5% | 10% | 10% |

**Reuse obligations were added for rigid packaging.** For rigid plastic packaging in the 0.9–4.9 litre or kilogramme band, brand owners face a minimum reuse target starting at 10% in FY 2025–26 and rising to 25% from FY 2028–29. For drinking water packaging of 4.9 litres or more, the target rises from 70% to 85% over the same period.

**Recycled plastic packaging must meet a standard.** It must conform to Indian Standard IS 14534:2023, carry labelling indicating recycled content, and comply with FSSAI marking requirements where the application involves food contact.

**Compliance was made softer at the edges.** Unfulfilled recycled content targets for food-contact packaging from FY 2025–26 can be carried forward for up to three years from FY 2026–27, provided at least one-third of the shortfall is cleared each year. Commentators have read this both ways — as a pragmatic transition and as a signal of tolerance for slippage.

## The definition change that may catch you

If you operate a processing yard, this is the paragraph to read twice.

The definition of "Plastic Waste Processors" has been broadened. It previously covered recyclers. It now covers recyclers *and* entities involved in end-of-life disposal — which the amendment defines as energy recovery, including co-processing in cement and steel plants, waste-to-energy, waste-to-oil, and use in road construction. Converting plastic waste into feedstock chemicals or new plastic is expressly carved out of "end-of-life disposal" and treated as recycling instead.

A new category of "seller" has also been introduced, covering anyone selling plastic raw material such as resins, pellets or intermediate material used to produce plastic packaging. That brings raw-material suppliers into the framework for the first time.

The practical effect is that a wider class of businesses now sits inside the regulatory perimeter than did before. If your operation touches plastic waste anywhere between collection and energy recovery, the sensible step is to check your registration status rather than assume the earlier position still holds.

Enforcement has also been decentralised. New sub-rules in Rule 12 assign responsibility over waste generator obligations and banned-item prohibitions to local bodies, gram panchayats and district-level panchayats, with a hierarchy for overlapping jurisdictions. State Level Monitoring Committees have been reconstituted under the Chief Secretary with broader membership. Verification may now be carried out by a "Registered Environment Auditor" as defined under the Environment Audit Rules, 2025, as an alternative to a designated agency, and the CPCB was given six months from notification to issue audit and verification guidelines.

## What this means commercially — and what it does not

The honest reading is that demand for recycled plastic should rise. A Category I target moving from 30% to 60% over four years has to be fed by somebody, and the domestic recycled plastics supply chain is not evenly developed across polymer grades. Commentary on the amendment has noted that this is likely to put real pressure on domestic supply.

But be careful about how you translate that into a business case, for three reasons.

**Quality is now specified, not assumed.** IS 14534:2023 conformity, recycled-content labelling and FSSAI marking for food contact mean the buyer of your output has a standard to hold you to. Volume alone will not clear the market.

**The certificate mechanism has a credibility history.** Importers cannot count recycled content in imported packaging towards their obligations and must instead buy certificates from PIBOs who have exceeded their targets, through a mechanism the CPCB is to develop on the centralised portal. Reporting on the earlier EPR credit system has pointed to serious integrity problems — the CPCB is reported to have found over six lakh fake certificates in 2023. Whether the new mechanism prices honestly is not yet knowable.

**Targets on paper are not tonnes in a yard.** Reported figures put annual plastic waste generation at around 4.13 million tonnes in 2022–23, against more than 20.7 million tonnes recycled cumulatively since 2022 under EPR — numbers that are self-reported through a central portal without comprehensive system-wide verification. Build your plan on contracts you can actually sign, not on national aggregates.

## Where machinery helps, and where it does not

This is the part most equipment suppliers will not tell you plainly, so here it is.

**A baler does not make recycled content.** Baling, shredding and sorting are preparation. They increase the density and consistency of material so it can be transported and fed economically into a recycling process. The recycled content target is met by the resin that comes out the other end, not by the bale.

What preparation equipment does change is your economics and your material quality — which is what determines whether a recycler will buy from you at all, and at what price.

## What to do in the next quarter

- **Confirm your registration category** against the widened "Plastic Waste Processor" definition with your State Pollution Control Board.
- **Watch for the CPCB audit and verification guidelines** due within six months of notification, and for the importer certificate mechanism on the centralised portal.
- **Check your output against IS 14534:2023** and the labelling requirements if you supply recycled packaging material.
- **Get your documentation in order** before an audit rather than during one — the amendment introduces the Registered Environment Auditor as a verification route.
- **Size equipment against contracts**, not against target tables.

Rules of this kind change often and are applied differently across states. Confirm the current position with the CPCB or your State Pollution Control Board, and take professional advice on your own compliance obligations.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2026 come into force?**
They were notified by the MoEFCC on 31 March 2026 through gazette notification G.S.R. 237(E) and came into force on publication in the Official Gazette. They amend the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016.

**What are the recycled content targets under the 2026 amendment?**
Category I rigid packaging rises from 30% in 2025–26 to 60% from 2028–29; Category II flexible packaging from 10% to 20%; and Category III multi-layered packaging from 5% to 10%. Confirm the current applicable figures with the CPCB, since targets have been revised repeatedly.

**Does the amendment change who counts as a plastic waste processor?**
Yes. The definition now covers both recyclers and entities involved in end-of-life disposal, such as waste-to-energy operators and co-processors. It previously covered recyclers only. A wider set of businesses is therefore inside the regulatory perimeter than before.

**Does buying a baling machine help meet EPR recycled content targets?**
Not directly. Balers, shredders and sorting lines prepare material — they raise density, improve consistency and cut transport cost per tonne. Recycled content targets are met by the recycled resin produced downstream. Preparation equipment improves the economics and quality of the material you supply into that chain.

**What standard must recycled plastic packaging meet?**
The amendment requires recycled plastic packaging to conform to Indian Standard IS 14534:2023, to carry labelling indicating recycled content, and to comply with FSSAI marking requirements for food-contact applications.
