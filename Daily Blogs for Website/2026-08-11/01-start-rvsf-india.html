How to Start a Vehicle Scrapping Business in India: Approvals, Site and Machinery

Most guides on how to start a vehicle scrapping business in India treat it as a licensing exercise — fill Form-1, get the certificate, buy a shear. That ordering is what sinks projects. The licence is the easy part. What determines whether the facility makes money is where you put it, what you can legally do with the material once it is dismantled, and whether the machinery matches the vehicle mix you can actually source.

What you are applying to become

The legal object is a Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility (RVSF) — a facility holding a certificate of registration under the Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules, 2021, which dismantles and scraps vehicles. The rules were published on 24 September 2021, came into force the following day, and were partially amended in September 2022 (Enviliance ASIA).

You are not applying to be a scrap dealer. An RVSF issues government-recognised documents — a Certificate of Deposit when a vehicle is handed over, and a digital Certificate of Vehicle Scrapping once it is destroyed — and those documents are what unlock the owner's incentives. That is the business you are entering: a compliance business that happens to produce steel.

Any legal entity may own and operate an RVSF provided it holds a Certificate of Incorporation, or a Shop and Establishment Act registration, or Udyam Aadhaar registration; a valid GST registration; and a valid PAN. Form-1, a registration fee and a security deposit go to the Registration Authority of the concerned State or Union Territory.

That last phrase does most of the work. The rules are central, the administration is state-level. Delhi, for example, issued its own implementation circular for the 2021 notification (Delhi Transport Department). Fee schedules, security deposit amounts, land-use rules and processing timelines vary. Get them from your own state transport department in writing.

The approval sequence, in the order that works

Applicants usually run these in parallel and get stuck. The dependencies matter.

1. Site control first. Not purchase — control. An option or lease-in-principle is enough. Every subsequent approval names a site, and changing the site mid-process restarts most of them.

2. Land use and building approval. Confirm the plot sits in a zone that permits industrial scrapping and dismantling, and get the site and building plan approved by the relevant municipal or town-planning authority. This is the most common place projects die, because a plot that is cheap is usually cheap for a zoning reason.

3. Pollution control board. Consent to Establish comes before you build; Consent to Operate before you run. Because an ELV yields waste oil, coolant, brake fluid, batteries and fuel, you will also need authorisation under the hazardous waste rules. Requirements vary by state board — treat your SPCB as the binding authority, not any summary you read online.

4. RVSF registration. Form-1 to the state Registration Authority with the fee and security deposit.

5. VAHAN integration. An RVSF must be able to access the VAHAN database to register vehicles for disposal and to issue the Certificate of Deposit and Certificate of Scrapping. Without working access you have a dismantling yard, not an RVSF.

6. ISO certification within twelve months. The rules require ISO 9001, ISO 14001 or ISO 45001 within 12 months of commencement of operations.

Registration runs for 10 years and may be extended by a further 10, subject to the audit and ISO requirements. The facility is audited by the Registration Authority or a designated body, and the report must be uploaded for each financial year by 31 May of the following year.

What the rules require you to do every day

This is the part that surprises people who come from the trade. An RVSF's obligations are operational, not paperwork-at-year-end:

  • Verify the vehicle against the National Crime Records Bureau stolen-vehicle database and with local police before scrapping it.
  • Verify the identity of the owner or their authorised representative and retain copies for at least six months.
  • Maintain records of every vehicle transaction, the scrap generated, its onward disposal to recyclers, and all equipment in the facility — available for inspection on demand.
  • Issue a digital Certificate of Vehicle Scrapping that includes a photograph of the detached chassis, and upload it to VAHAN.
  • Run CCTV at the scrapping yard and reception, with records kept for three months.

Budget for this. A compliance clerk, a working IT setup, reliable connectivity and a camera system that actually retains footage are line items, not overheads to be absorbed.

On the physical side, the policy statement announcing the programme said scrapping centres must comply with environmental and pollution norms and have adequate parking, de-pollution equipment for air, water and sound pollution, and adequate facilities for hazardous waste management and disposal (PIB). In practice the parking requirement drives land area more than the machinery does — vehicles arrive in batches and sit while documentation clears.

Where the money comes from, and where it leaks

Be clear-eyed here, because this is where business plans get written on hope.

Revenue is mostly the material. Ferrous body steel is the volume; the margin sits in the non-ferrous fractions — aluminium, copper harness, catalytic converters where applicable — plus reusable parts where your state permits resale, and tyres, glass and batteries routed to authorised recyclers. Non-ferrous rates move daily and by grade: published Delhi market rates on 10 August 2026 showed aluminium purja (engine import) around ₹249/kg and copper armature around ₹1,386/kg, against far lower per-kg ferrous rates (Shree Metal Prices). That is one day in one market, not a planning assumption — but it shows why separation discipline, not throughput, decides your margin.

Acquisition cost is your biggest variable. The policy framework set the scrap value paid to the owner at approximately 4–6% of the ex-showroom price of a new vehicle, alongside a proposed road tax rebate of up to 25% for personal and 15% for commercial vehicles, a 5% manufacturer discount against the scrapping certificate, and a possible registration-fee waiver (PIB). Those incentives are what make an owner choose a registered facility over the informal market. They are also state- and manufacturer-dependent and have been revised over time — verify the current position with MoRTH and your state transport department before you model them.

The leaks are predictable: vehicles idling because documentation is incomplete, mixed fractions sold at the lowest common rate because dismantling was rushed, and de-pollution shortcuts that turn into a consent problem. None of these are machinery problems.

You do not need all of them on day one, and any supplier who tells you otherwise is selling a package rather than solving a problem.

A defensible order for a first facility:

  1. De-pollution station and material handling. Non-negotiable, and required for consent regardless of tonnage.
  2. A cutting machine. An alligator shear is the flexible first choice — it sizes round bar, channel, TMT, angle, cables, plates and tubes, needs no civil foundation, and lets you sell sized material immediately.
  3. A press. Which one depends on what your buyer wants. A car baler if you are selling whole-vehicle bundles; a high density or triple action baler if you are selling separated body steel. Ask your buyer before you choose.
  4. Casting cracker and shredder. Add these once engine and gearbox volumes justify them.

Frequently asked questions

What licence do I need to start a vehicle scrapping business in India? A certificate of registration as a Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility under the Motor Vehicles (Registration and Functions of Vehicle Scrapping Facility) Rules, 2021, granted by the Registration Authority of your state or union territory. Alongside it you will need pollution control board consents, hazardous waste authorisation and approved land use. Requirements and fees are administered at state level, so confirm the current position with your own state transport department.

How long is an RVSF registration valid? Ten years, extendable by a further ten years provided the registered scrapper complies with the audit and ISO certification requirements. The annual audit report must be uploaded for each financial year by 31 May of the following year.

Do I need ISO certification before I can operate? No. The rules require ISO 9001, ISO 14001 or ISO 45001 within 12 months of commencement of operation. Begin the gap assessment during commissioning rather than near the deadline.

How much land does a vehicle scrapping facility need? There is no single national figure that applies everywhere, and any number quoted without reference to your state is guesswork. Size the plot from three things: batch parking for vehicles awaiting documentation, a covered de-pollution and dismantling area, and machine footprint plus material movement. Parking usually dominates. Confirm the minimum with your state transport department and pollution control board.

Which machine should an RVSF buy first? After de-pollution and material handling, a shear is usually the most flexible first purchase because it sizes mixed structural material and needs no civil foundation. The press comes second, chosen against what your buyer wants to receive — whole-vehicle bundles point to a car baler, separated body steel to a high density or triple action baler.

Is the vehicle scrapping business profitable? It can be, but the margin sits in separation quality and acquisition cost, not throughput. Model it yourself: take a realistic monthly vehicle count, apply a conservative recovery split by material, price each fraction at current local trade rates, then subtract acquisition cost, compliance staffing, power and disposal charges for hazardous fractions. Anyone quoting a standard payback period without those inputs is guessing.
