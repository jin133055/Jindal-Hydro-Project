# JHP SEO Strategy — Competitor Audit & Ranking Roadmap

**Jindal Hydro Projects Inc. · Prepared 3 August 2026**

---

## The finding that changes everything

**JHP does not have an owned website.**

Both domains resolve to third-party marketplace microsites:

| Domain | What it actually is | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| `jindalhydro.com` | Redirects to `jindalhydraulics.com` — an **IndiaMART seller microsite** | IndiaMART InterMESH Ltd. |
| `jindalhydroprojects.com` | A **TradeIndia seller microsite** | Infocom Network Pvt. Ltd. |

Neither can host a blog. Neither lets you control URLs, add schema markup, set canonical tags, or build internal linking. On the IndiaMART site, every product lives at an anchor fragment (`page.html#product-name`) rather than its own URL — meaning **no individual product can rank in Google at all**. The TradeIndia site is better in that respect (each product has its own `.html` page), but the copy is templated boilerplate shared across thousands of seller catalogues, which Google treats as low-value duplicate content.

Both sites also still use `<meta name="keywords">`, a tag Google stopped using for ranking in 2009.

### What this means

The 261-keyword tracker, the content gap analysis and the blog programme are all valid and ready. But **they cannot be executed until there is a site to publish on**. Blogs written today have nowhere to live.

**Recommendation: build an owned website. This is the single highest-return action available, and nothing else in this document works without it.** A static site (the approach your closest competitor uses) is inexpensive, fast, and sufficient — this is not an argument for an expensive rebuild.

Keep the IndiaMART and TradeIndia listings running. They generate enquiries and cost little. But they are lead-gen channels, not an SEO asset.

## Competitive landscape

Thirteen sites audited. Three distinct groups.

### Group 1 — The marketplaces (cannot be beaten head-on)

**IndiaMART** and **TradeIndia** occupy most of page one for nearly every transactional head term: "scrap baling press machine", "hydraulic baling press", "scrap baler machine price". Their domain authority is vastly beyond what any manufacturer can build.

**Strategic response: do not fight them on head terms.** Outflank them. Directories cannot publish how-to guides, specification explainers, comparison articles, troubleshooting content or policy analysis. That is where a manufacturer wins, and where the buyer actually forms an opinion before they ever search a transactional term.

### Group 2 — Indian manufacturers with owned sites

| Competitor | Owned site | Blog | Posts | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jain Hydraulics** | Yes (modern static) | Yes | ~8 | **HIGH** |
| Advance Hydrautech | Yes (WordPress) | Yes | ~20 | Medium |
| Kelvin Water Technologies | Yes (WordPress) | Yes | ~30 | Medium |
| JMD Industries | Yes | Yes | ~10 | Medium |
| Shreeji Machinery | Yes | No | 0 | Medium |
| Mask Hydraulics | Yes | No | 0 | Low |

**Jain Hydraulics is the one to watch.** They have done the thing JHP has not: a proper owned domain with a clean product taxonomy (`/products/balers/triple-action`, `/products/shears/alligator`), a real blog, two service locations (Delhi + Pune), WhatsApp contact, and active YouTube and Instagram.

But their content is thin — roughly eight short posts. And critically, they have **no RVSF/ELV positioning and no agriculture range**. Those are JHP's two strongest differentiators.

### Group 3 — International reference points

**PRAB (US)** is the benchmark for how this category should be marketed online. Their meta descriptions carry hard numbers ("capture up to 99% of coolant", "ROI within one year"), they maintain a deep resource library, use proper Article schema with published and modified timestamps, and embed video. Not a competitor for Indian buyers — but the model to copy.

**Sinobaler (China)** ranks well internationally on generic baler terms and leads with capacity specs. **WEIMA (Germany)** demonstrates premium technical depth across multiple languages.

## Where JHP can win

Twelve content gaps identified. The top six, ranked by opportunity:

### 1. RVSF and ELV machinery guidance — opportunity 95/100

Almost no Indian machinery manufacturer publishes serious content on setting up a vehicle scrapping facility. Search demand is rising with policy enforcement, intent is strongly commercial, and the searcher is by definition about to buy a full plant's worth of equipment.

JHP has a purpose-built car baler and offers turnkey RVSF plants. **Nobody else in this market is claiming this ground.** This is the single biggest opportunity in the whole analysis.

### 2. Machine specification deep-dives — opportunity 90/100

Every competitor publishes spec tables. Nobody explains what the numbers mean. What does 650 tons of cylinder force actually let you compact? Why does cycle time matter more than press force for throughput? What is the practical difference between HARDOX 400 and 500-BHN liner plates?

Buyers search these questions constantly and find nothing useful. This content also feeds AI answer engines, which increasingly matter as much as blue links.

### 3. Comparison content — opportunity 88/100

"Triple action vs double action", "baler vs shredder", "vertical vs horizontal", "new vs used". Extremely low difficulty, high buying intent, and **zero Indian manufacturers publish honest comparisons** because they are afraid of talking a customer out of a machine. That fear is the opportunity — the manufacturer who explains trade-offs honestly gets trusted, and gets the enquiry.

### 4. Spares, service and AMC — opportunity 85/100

"Baling press spare parts", "baler shear blade replacement", "hydraulic power pack for baler". Very high commercial intent, almost no competition. JHP holds 50,000+ spares — a genuine advantage nobody is marketing. These searches also capture owners of *competitors'* machines.

### 5. Agriculture waste machinery — opportunity 82/100

Fodder block and straw baling content is thin, and stubble-burning policy drives large seasonal search volume every autumn. Jain Hydraulics has no agriculture range at all.

### 6. Local city pages — opportunity 78/100

Nobody has built city-level landing pages for scrap machinery in India. Twenty-eight local keywords in the tracker at difficulty 8–25. Low volume individually, but they convert and they are nearly free to win.

## Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Build the site (blocking)

Nothing below works without this.

- Register or reclaim a primary domain and build an owned site
- Product taxonomy: `/products/<category>/<machine>` — one URL per machine, no anchor fragments
- Blog at `/blog/<slug>`
- Solutions section: `/solutions/rvsf-turnkey`, `/industries/<vertical>`
- `/spares` and `/service` pages
- Google Search Console and Analytics verified
- 301-redirect strategy so existing marketplace traffic and any inbound links are preserved

Keep the IndiaMART and TradeIndia listings live throughout — they are working lead channels.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4: foundation

- Migrate all 25 machines from the catalogue onto individual product pages with full specs, materials processed and suitable industries
- Product schema + FAQ schema on every product page
- Publish 2 blogs/day (Mon–Sat) + 1 deep dive (Sunday) — the pipeline is already built
- Prioritise the RVSF cluster and comparison content first
- Set the baseline positions in the Rank Tracker

### Phase 2 — Weeks 5–12: authority

- Build out the informational cluster (all 28 keywords)
- Publish the spares and service pages with real part-level content
- City landing pages for the top 10 locations
- Case studies with actual throughput and payback numbers — **nobody in this market does this**
- Start link building: industry associations (MRAI), trade publications, supplier and customer sites

### Phase 3 — Months 4–6: expansion

- Agriculture cluster ahead of the autumn stubble season
- Export-market landing pages
- Video on product pages with VideoObject schema
- Review and refresh the earliest posts

## Technical SEO checklist for the new site

- One URL per product; no anchor-fragment navigation
- Unique title (≤60 chars) and meta description (120–160 chars) on every page
- Canonical tags everywhere
- Product, Organization, BreadcrumbList, FAQPage and BlogPosting schema
- OG images at 1200×630 (both marketplace sites currently serve 120×120)
- `summary_large_image` Twitter cards
- XML sitemap + robots.txt
- Core Web Vitals: images in WebP, lazy loading, minimal JS
- Mobile-first — the majority of Indian B2B search is mobile
- HTTPS with HSTS
- Internal linking: every blog links to 2–3 product pages; every product page links to related guides
- Remove `<meta name="keywords">` — obsolete and a mild spam signal
- `hreflang` if export-market pages are added

## Measuring it

Track in `JHP_SEO_Keyword_Tracker.xlsx` weekly.

**Leading indicators** (move in weeks): keywords with any position, average position, keywords in the 11–20 band, indexed pages, impressions.

**Lagging indicators** (move in months): keywords in top 10, organic clicks, enquiries from organic, enquiry rate.

Realistic expectations for a new domain: little movement for 8–12 weeks, long-tail and local terms landing first, informational terms from roughly month 3, and competitive commercial terms from month 6 onward. Head terms owned by IndiaMART and TradeIndia may never be winnable — and do not need to be.

## The one-line version

JHP has a deeper product range, five decades of manufacturing, and two categories (RVSF/ELV and agriculture) that no Indian competitor covers — and no website to put any of it on. Build the site, and a 2-post-a-day cadence out-covers every Indian competitor within a month.
